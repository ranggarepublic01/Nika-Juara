## Language & sound (decided 7 Oct)
- Bahasa Indonesia first. Chorus = short chant (2–4 words, call-and-response), verses = plain concrete everyday images (warung TV, syal, Bapak) in the style of current Indonesian indie/pop hits (Hindia, Nadhif Basalamah, Tulus, Perunggu, Last Child). Avoid slogan-only lines.
- English only as a hook word ("Garuda Calling") or a separate version of a flagship.
- Sound: keep Nika's epic brand → "epic cinematic arena rock" (orchestra + choir + war drums + arena-rock stomp and guitars + gamelan accents + crowd chants).
- Two fixed voices: MALE lead = powerful gritty baritone-tenor, raspy chest voice, punchy rhythmic verses, shouted anthemic belts (Persona). FEMALE counter-voice = warm dark classical mezzo-soprano, operatic, soaring sustained lines, used for intros, bridges, final lifts, and as lead on the sad tracks (#1, #2). Artist names can't go in Suno prompts, so describe the traits only.
- Base style string: epic cinematic arena rock, orchestral strings and brass, epic choir, heavy stomp-clap percussion and taiko war drums, distorted guitars, subtle gamelan accents, stadium crowd chants, powerful gritty raspy male baritone-tenor lead, warm operatic mezzo-soprano female counter-vocal, anthemic, triumphant
- Build the male Persona from a Pro-made song (JUARA!), never from free-plan Garuda Calling. Check in the Suno app whether making a Persona from a song is still possible (reports from Jul 2026 say Suno may now only allow uploading your own voice). Fallback: one locked style string reused on every track.
- Garuda Calling: Reborn = same lyrics, fresh Pro generation (no cover, no Suno support request needed).

## Production rules for every album track (learned on JUARA!)
- Tested and dropped (8 Oct): forcing the instrumental riff to play the chorus melody did not work in Suno. Use normal structure tags (instrumental intro, build-up, drop, melodic interlude, instrumental break, breakdown).
- Hook in the first 20 seconds; no long instrumental intro.
- Pronunciation: Suno can read Indonesian words as English, especially at the very start with no context ("Ayo" came out as "eyo"). In the Suno lyrics box spell sounds phonetically and put "Indonesian" in the tag; keep the normal spelling in on-screen lyrics and video titles.
- Abbreviations are ALWAYS written as Indonesian letter sounds in the Suno lyrics box, never as capitals: GBK → Ge-Be-Ka, PSSI → Pe-Es-Es-I, AFC → A-Ef-Ce, TV → Ti-Vi. Capital letters get read out in English ("jee-bee-kay").
- Known phonetic fixes: Ayo → Ah-yo (when it opens a song or section without context).
- Fix single bad sections with Replace Section instead of rerolling the whole song.
- Model: v6 mini on Pro gave the best verse delivery for JUARA!.
